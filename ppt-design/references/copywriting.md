# Copywriting for Slides

Slide copy is not prose. It's telegraphic — the audience is listening to you, not
reading.

## The core principle

> "Intervention reduced costs by 23% (p < 0.01)"
> not "Our study found that the intervention reduced costs significantly."

**Headlines ≤6–8 words. Action titles ≤15 words and ≤2 lines.**

## Action titles vs topic labels

The single highest-leverage copy change.

| Topic label (banned) | Action title |
|---|---|
| Results | Intervention reduced costs by 23% |
| Market overview | Three entrants took 40% share in 18 months |
| Literature Review | Prior work assumes stationarity; ours does not |
| Conclusion | Ship the pipeline, defer the UI |

**The ghost-deck test catches topic labels:** write the deck as plain text, one
line per slide. If a line is a topic label, the content isn't thought through
yet.

## The copywriting formulas

### PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)
**Use:** problem slides, pain points
**Template:** "[Pain point]? Every [time frame], [consequence]. [Solution] fixes
this."

### AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
**Use:** CTAs, closing slides
**Template:** "[Bold statement]. [Benefit detail]. [Social proof]. [CTA]."

### FAB (Features-Advantages-Benefits)
**Use:** feature slides, product showcases
**Template:** "[Feature] lets you [advantage], so you can [benefit]."

### Cost of Inaction
**Use:** agitation slides, urgency
**Template:** "Without [solution], you're losing [amount] every [timeframe]."

### Before-After-Bridge
**Use:** transformation slides, case studies
**Template:** "[Pain point before]. [Desired state after]. [Your solution] is the
bridge."

## Formula-to-slide mapping

| Slide type | Primary formula | Emotion |
|---|---|---|
| Title / hook | AIDA, Hook | curiosity |
| Problem | PAS, Agitate | frustration |
| Cost / risk | Cost of Inaction | fear |
| Solution | FAB, BAB | hope |
| Features | FAB | confidence |
| Proof | Before-After-Bridge | trust |
| Closing | AIDA | resolve |

## The deck structure

A narrative arc, not a table of contents.

```
1. Title          — the claim, in one line. Dark.
2. Section divider
3. The problem    — why the audience should care
4. Cost of inaction — what happens if nothing changes
5. The approach   — what you did, in one sentence
6. How it works   — the mechanism, one slide
7. Evidence       — the numbers, one chart
8. What it means  — interpretation
9. Limitations    — what it doesn't do (builds trust)
10. Conclusions   — what to do next. Dark. Stays up for Q&A.
```

**End on conclusions, not "Thank You."** The conclusions slide stays on screen
during Q&A. Backup slides come after an appendix divider, excluded from the page
count.

## Density rules

- **One idea per slide.** Sequential points become sequential slides.
- **≤6 bullets; 3–5 typical.**
- **≤40 words of body text per content slide.**
- **Max 2 accent cards per slide**; demote the rest to plain text.
- **Density floors as well as ceilings.** ≤3 short text-only bullets is too
  sparse. Pure text-only slides ≤20–30% of the deck.
- **Content-ratio target:** visual 60–70%, charts/diagrams 10–15%, text 15–20%.

## Bold and italic

**Sparingly.** Bold for:
- Key terms on first introduction
- Inline labels ("Note:", "Limitation:")
- The focal finding

Italic for:
- Statistical notation
- Species names
- Book and paper titles

**Never for decoration.**

## Speaker notes

**Telegraphic — 3–5 talking points per slide:**
1. The key message
2. The transition to the next slide
3. Likely questions

**Never a full script.** Opt-in, not on by default.

## The register decides everything

| Register | Tone | Type | Motion | Density |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Keynote** (TED) | declarative, bold | oversized, one family | dramatic | very low |
| **Academic** | precise, hedged correctly | serif display, one family | minimal | medium |
| **Consulting** (McKinsey) | action-titled, structured | sans, tight | subtle | high |
| **Investor** | confident, numeric | clean sans | subtle | medium |
| **Internal** | direct, practical | system sans | minimal | high |

**Ask which register before writing a word.** The wrong register is the most
common reason a technically-fine deck lands badly.

## The integrity rule

> **Never invent a number, name, or word.**

One tool in the source set printed *43% growth* where the real figure was *12%*.
Every figure traces to source material. If a number isn't available, the slide
says so.

Export is *blocked* (not warned) when structure, text occupancy, number
provenance, or page bounds fail.

## Common copy failures

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Topic-label titles | write the finding as a sentence |
| Bullets in a keynote register | one idea per slide |
| "Our study found that…" | state the finding directly |
| Centered body paragraphs | left-align |
| Hedged conclusions ("may potentially") | commit, or state the limitation explicitly |
| Jargon the audience doesn't share | plain language; fewer steps |
| A slide that needs narration to make sense | it fails the 10-second test |
